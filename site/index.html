What is the SenCat score?

SenCat is a published gene-expression signature of cellular senescence, the lasting growth arrest that builds up as cells age in culture. It was derived from many senescent and normal human cell types (Anerillas et al., Mol Cell 2026). The score weighs genes that rise or fall in senescent cells. Read it within one donor over time: higher means a more senescence-like profile, not a failed batch.

    How it works

    1. 1Check

      Gene names, number format, coverage, sequencing depth, donor identity and study design. Each check exists because of a real error found in public data.

    2. 2Normalize

      Puts samples sequenced to different depths on the same footing (median of ratios, verified against PyDESeq2). FPKM is rescaled to TPM.

    3. 3Score

      Applies the published SenCat gene weights, fetched at a fixed version and checksum-verified on load.

    4. 4Follow each donor

      Compares each donor with its own earliest sample. Scores from different labs or media are never put on one absolute scale.

    5. 5Forecast and challenge

      A drift model learned across cohorts forecasts later passages. Random gene sets and a held-out clock act as controls.

    Words used on this page
    MSC
    Mesenchymal stromal cells, grown in the lab from bone marrow, fat, umbilical cord or other tissue.
    Passage (P)
    One round of splitting a culture into new flasks. P3 means the cells have been split three times.
    Donor
    The person the cells came from. Each donor is compared with itself.
    Cell bank
    Frozen stock of early cells (master or working bank) that later cultures are grown from.
    Senescence
    A lasting stop in cell division that builds up with age and repeated culture.
    RNA-seq
    Sequencing that measures the activity (expression) of all genes at once. It is the reference method here.
    qPCR
    A faster, cheaper test that measures a small set of chosen genes.
    Z-score (heatmap colour)
    How far one sample sits from that gene's average across the samples shown, in standard deviations.
    Forecast
    A model estimate of later passages from an early sample. It is not a measurement.
