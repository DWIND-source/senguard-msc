    How it works

    1. 1Check

      Ids, scale, transformation, coverage, depth, identity and design. Each check exists because of a failure met in public data.

    2. 2Normalize

      Median of ratios for counts, verified against PyDESeq2. FPKM is rescaled to TPM.

    3. 3Score

      Published SenCat weights, fetched at a pinned commit and checksum-verified on load.

    4. 4Follow each donor

      Slope and rank correlation against passage, never pooled onto one absolute scale.

    5. 5Challenge it

      Random signatures as a control, plus a drift clock trained with each cohort held out.

    What "twin" means here

    A digital record of one culture series, built only from its measured expression. You can move through its passages, inspect any sample, and see where it sits in expression space.

    What it doesn't do

    It doesn't simulate cells or predict potency. We also tested forecasting the next passage, and it didn't pass, so it is switched off.

    The same analysis runs in the validated Python pipeline, and automated tests compare the two: scores agree within 1e-13. Code, protocols and result tables.

    What it cannot tell you

    • Not potencyNothing here measures T-cell suppression, immune function, therapeutic effect or safety.
    • Not a release testNo threshold has been validated. A rising score doesn't mean a lot has failed, and a flat one doesn't mean it is fine.
    • Not an assayPublic MSC cohorts rarely have per-sample senescence assays, so this reports trends and not accuracy.
    • Partly time in culturePassage, culture time and possibly batch move together in these datasets.
